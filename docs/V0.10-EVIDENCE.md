# V0.10 — telemetry evidence

Two questions V0.9 left open, re-asked against every persisted session on the
development machine rather than against a single car.

**Corpus.** 45 recorded sessions, 1.45 GB. 11 are `TelemetryFrame` schema 2 and
therefore carry wheel telemetry; the other 34 are schema 1, which has no wheel
group at all. The schema-2 corpus is 399,662 frames (389,543 active) across two
vehicles, `3520` and `3631`, including one continuous 78-minute drive.

**Tools.** Both are read-only examples that open `manifest.json` and
`frames.rlframes` and write nothing:

```bash
cargo run --release --manifest-path src-tauri/Cargo.toml --example slip_portability -- '<sessions-root>' --json report.json
```

```bash
cargo run --release --manifest-path src-tauri/Cargo.toml --example deferred_fields -- '<sessions-root>'
```

Raw output is in `docs/v0.10-evidence/`.

---

## 1. Cross-car slip portability

### Question

V0.9 set `slip_ratio_enter = 1.0` and `combined_slip_enter = 1.0` as absolute,
dimensionless thresholds and recorded the known risk that they might be
properties of the car they were chosen on. Is the absolute threshold portable?

### Method

Per vehicle and per corner, over active frames only: the distribution of
`|slip_ratio|` and `combined_slip`, and the **share of analyzed time** spent at
or above the current threshold. Time-weighted, not sample-counted, using the
same `max_gap_ms` exclusion the analyzer applies — a sample count would let a
high-rate session outvote a low-rate one.

### Result

Share of analyzed time above the current absolute thresholds, worst corner:

| Vehicle | Sessions | Analyzed | `\|slip_ratio\|` ≥ 1.0 | `combined_slip` ≥ 1.0 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3520 | 5 | 329 s | 17.5 % | 21.1 % |
| 3631 | 6 | 5,086 s | 30.2 % | **67.5 %** |

Worst-corner distributions:

| Vehicle | Channel | p50 | p90 | p95 | p99 | max |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3520 | `\|slip_ratio\|` | 0.111 | 4.282 | 16.278 | 46.279 | 123.284 |
| 3520 | `combined_slip` | 0.150 | 4.861 | 16.447 | 46.468 | 123.284 |
| 3631 | `\|slip_ratio\|` | 0.985 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 38.522 | 100.642 |
| 3631 | `combined_slip` | **1.163** | 1.414 | 1.414 | 38.580 | 100.754 |

The decisive row is vehicle 3631's `combined_slip`: its **median is above the
threshold**. A threshold that more than half the drive sits above is not
selecting events; it is describing the baseline.

Per corner, 3631 is asymmetric in a way 3520 is not. Its rear-right
`slip_ratio` has p50 = 0.984 and p90 = p95 = 1.000 — pinned at exactly 1.0 — and
its rear-right `combined_slip` p90 = p95 = 1.414, which is `hypot(1.0, 1.0)` to
three decimals, consistent with the V0.8 finding that `combined_slip ==
hypot(slip_ratio, slip_angle)`. Its front-left `slip_ratio` is 0.000 at both p50
and p90. The other three of 3520's corners cluster within a factor of two of
each other.

Independent corroboration from the analyzer itself: the 78-minute session from
vehicle 3631 produces **8 slip episodes across 4,704 analyzed seconds**. The
V0.9 coalescing rule is merging near-continuous above-threshold slip into a
handful of very long episodes — exactly the degeneracy `docs/V0.9-ANALYSIS.md`
predicted when it wrote that `slip_episode_seconds` read against
`analyzed_seconds` is "the honest way to see that a session of near-continuous
wheelspin is being reported as a handful of long episodes".

### Finding

**The current absolute slip threshold is not portable across vehicles.** The
same value selects 21 % of one car's driving and 67 % of the other's — a factor
of 7.8 between their worst-corner medians (0.150 against 1.163).

### Decision: keep the existing semantics unchanged

Two vehicles establish that the threshold is *not* portable. They do not
establish what a portable rule would be, and nothing here licenses inventing
one:

- A per-vehicle normalization would require asserting a physical meaning for
  `slip_ratio`. Its normalization domain is not established — that is precisely
  why V0.8 gave it no SI name — so any normalization would be invented.
- A percentile-relative threshold ("the top 5 % of this car's slip") would make
  every session's events depend on the rest of that session, so a clean lap and
  a scrappy one would be scored against different rules. That is a larger
  product decision than V0.10 is scoped for.
- Retuning the absolute value on two cars would repeat the original mistake with
  a different number.

`slip_ratio_enter` and `combined_slip_enter` therefore stay at 1.0, slip stays
presented as coalesced episodes, and the terminology stays neutral: a high slip
episode, never wheelspin, lock, traction loss or a slide.

### Limitation carried forward to V1.0

The slip thresholds are **RaceLab product definitions whose selectivity varies
by an order of magnitude between vehicles.** A session reporting few, long slip
episodes may mean the car spent most of the drive above the threshold rather
than that little happened. `slip_episode_seconds` against
`coverage.analyzed_seconds` is the ratio that distinguishes the two, and both
are already in every analysis document.

Resolving this needs a corpus of **at least six to eight distinct vehicles
spanning drivetrain layouts and power levels**, which the evidence tooling added
here now makes a mechanical exercise rather than a research one.

## 2. Deferred telemetry audit

### Question

V0.8 deferred `boost`, `fuel`, `distance_traveled`, `best_lap`, `last_lap`,
`current_lap` and the raw `gear` code on 14,237 active packets from one car. Can
any of them now be promoted?

The bar is unchanged: a field exists in `TelemetryFrame` only once its *meaning*
and its *unit* are supported by evidence.

### Corpus note

2.17 % of active frames (8,454) are event-mode frames, identified by
`race_position > 0`. The remainder is free roam. Several deferred fields are
populated only in event mode, which is itself a finding: V0.8 saw them as
permanently zero because its captures contained no events.

### Field by field

| Field | V0.8 said | V0.10 measures (389,543 active frames) | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `boost` (284) | −11.02 … 24.99, saturates at 24.989952 | **−14.70 … 0.000**, mean −13.64, 93.1 % negative, pinned at exactly 0.0 in 6.87 %; r(throttle, boost) = +0.868 | **Defer** |
| `fuel` (288) | constant 1.0 | constant 1.0 in every one of 389,543 frames | **Defer** |
| `distance_traveled` (292) | constant 0.0 | **−70.81 … 5952.13**, non-zero in 2.17 %, 25 decreases, can be negative | **Defer** |
| `best_lap` (296) | constant 0.0 | constant 0.0, including all 8,454 event frames | **Defer** |
| `last_lap` (300) | constant 0.0 | constant 0.0, including all 8,454 event frames | **Defer** |
| `current_lap` (304) | constant 0.0 | 0 in 97.83 %, max 111.05, non-zero only in event frames | **Defer** |
| `gear` (319) | codes 0 and 11 seen, no mapping | ordered ratio clusters per vehicle; code 0 established as reverse | **Defer** |

### `boost` — the V0.8 range does not reproduce

Across two different cars the field never goes positive. It floors at exactly
−14.700000 and pins at exactly 0.0 under load, with a strong positive
correlation to throttle. That is the shape of a manifold-pressure reading on
naturally aspirated cars, where "boost" reads vacuum and tops out at ambient.

−14.7 is suggestive: 14.696 psi is one standard atmosphere, so a psi-gauge
reading would floor at about −14.7 at perfect vacuum. **A clamp value matching a
physical constant is a hint, not a unit.** Promoting on it would mean asserting
psi on the strength of one coincidence, with no independent reading to check
against.

*What would settle it:* drive a turbocharged car with the game's own boost
readout visible and compare a held-boost value against offset 284 in the same
frame. One car with a positive range and one matching reading establishes both
sign convention and unit.

### `fuel` — reconfirmed, with much stronger evidence

Constant 1.0 across 389,543 frames, two vehicles and roughly two hours,
including a continuous 78-minute drive. A field that never moves over that
corpus is positive evidence that it is not a consumption fraction in these
modes, not merely absent evidence for one.

### `distance_traveled` — V0.8's key claim does not reproduce

V0.8 recorded "exactly 0.0 in every active packet, including long free-roam
drives" and treated that as positive evidence against the field. It is non-zero
in 2.17 % of this corpus, ranging to 5952.13, and it goes **negative** (down to
−70.81).

A negative value rules out a simple odometer. The field is populated only
alongside `race_position` and `current_lap`, so it is event-scoped, and its
semantics — distance into an event, distance to something, a signed track
offset — remain unestablished.

`SessionSummaryV1.distance_meters` therefore stays the speed-integrated value.
The V0.8 reasoning that the two quantities do not mean the same thing is
unaffected.

### `best_lap` / `last_lap` — absence of evidence, not evidence of absence

Both are exactly 0.0 in every frame, *including* every event frame. But
`lap_number` is also constant 0 throughout the corpus, so no lap was ever
completed in any recorded session — consistent with Horizon events that are
point-to-point rather than lapped. The corpus cannot distinguish "these fields
are always zero" from "no lap has ever finished in front of them".

*What would settle it:* record a circuit race of at least three laps.

### `current_lap` — the unit test does not have enough data

`race_time_seconds` was promoted in V0.8 on the argument that it advances 1:1
with the game timestamp. That test is re-run here as its own control, and
`current_lap` is judged by the same standard.

The comparison must be made **over a span, never per frame**: both clocks are
f32 seconds, and at a race time of 4,700 s an f32 ULP is about 0.24 ms against a
13 ms frame interval, so a single interval's ratio is pure quantization. Runs
also have to break at inactive frames, because a menu advances the packet
timestamp while a race clock is stopped.

With both corrections:

| Clock | Runs within 1e−3 of 1:1 | Span | Worst \|ratio − 1\| |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `current_race_time` (308), the control | 11 / 17 | 5,407 s | 1.4 × 10⁻² |
| `current_lap` (304), the candidate | 1 / 2 | 117 s | 8.4 × 10⁻³ |

The candidate has **two usable runs over 117 seconds**. That is far too little to
establish anything, and it cannot be improved without event-mode recordings. The
control's own 6 non-matching runs are within 1.4 % and are consistent with short
spans containing a pause the active flag did not mark; they are not evidence
against the promoted field.

### `gear` — the strongest new evidence, and still not enough

A gear code can be established without trusting its name, by showing that it
partitions the speed/RPM ratio into ordered, separated clusters. That is a
physical consequence, not an assumption.

**Pooling the two vehicles destroys the signal** — their gearboxes differ — and
the tool reports both so the difference is visible. Per vehicle, comparing only
*consecutive* codes:

| Vehicle | Codes | Consecutive steps | Ratio medians |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| pooled | 0–8, 11 | 7 | **not monotonic** |
| 3520 | 0–8, 11 | 7 | **strictly increasing** |
| 3631 | 0–6, 11 | 5 | **strictly increasing** |

Vehicle 3631, codes 2 → 6: ratio medians 0.003017, 0.004794, 0.006211, 0.007788,
0.009613, with the p05–p95 spread tightening to **0.038** at codes 5 and 6 and
speed ranges that barely overlap (32–58, 44–67, 56–88, 70–91 m/s). That is what
a fixed gear ratio looks like.

**Code 0 is established as reverse.** Among moving frames under code 0, summed
wheel rotation is negative in 158 of 186 (3520) and 673 of 730 (3631). This uses
only `Wheel::rotation_rad_s`, which V0.8 established as signed with forward
rotation positive, so it needs no world-axis convention — deriving direction
from `velocity` and `orientation` would require asserting an axis handedness
that no RaceLab evidence supports.

What is *not* established:

- **Code 1** carries 93.7 % of all frames with a p05–p95 spread of 7.1 (3520) and
  10.6 (3631), and its p95 ratio exceeds top gear's. It also contains reversing
  frames — 32 % of its moving frames on 3631. It is not purely "first gear".
- **Code 11** is rare (0.09–0.64 %), forward-only, and has a wide spread (1.17,
  1.69) sitting between other gears' ratios. It is not a fixed ratio.

A canonical `Gear` must map every observed code. Two of ten are unestablished,
so `gear` stays `None` for FH6 and the raw code stays in Diagnostics, labelled a
code. `Gear::Unmapped` exists for exactly this and is deliberately still not
used here: populating it would put a meaningless number in front of a user for
no gain.

*What would settle it:* a manual drive in one car through every forward gear and
reverse with the in-game gear indicator visible, recorded, then compared against
offset 319 frame by frame. That resolves codes 1 and 11 directly.

### Result

**No field is promoted. `TelemetryFrame` stays at schema 2, unchanged.**

No V3 was created, no field was added to be filled later, and three of V0.8's
stated observations (`boost` range, `distance_traveled` constancy, and the
corpus having no event-mode frames) are corrected here rather than carried
forward. Unknown remains unknown.
