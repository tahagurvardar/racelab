# V0.8 — canonical telemetry expansion: evidence and validation

This is the evidence record for the fields promoted into
[`TelemetryFrame` schema v2](V0.8-TELEMETRY-SCHEMA.md), and the reasons every
other candidate was deferred.

**V0.8 is not frozen.** Everything below is derived from offline analysis of
recorded packets. No claim here has been confirmed against a running copy of
FH6; the [manual acceptance plan](#manual-fh6-acceptance-plan) is what does
that, and it has not been performed.

## Why each promoted field is promoted

### Power and torque are watts and newton-metres

Power and torque are related by `P = τ·ω`. Reading offset 260 as watts, offset
264 as newton-metres and RPM as the angular velocity, that identity holds:

| | relative error |
|---|---|
| median | 2.227e-4 |
| p90 | 1.383e-3 |
| p99 | 8.459e-3 |
| worst | 5.068e-1 |

over 14,200 samples where `|τω| > 1000 W`. A median agreement of 0.02% is not
compatible with any other unit pair: kilowatts would be wrong by a factor of
1000 and horsepower by a factor of 1.34.

The tail is explained rather than ignored. Frames where the relative error
exceeds 1e-3 have a mean `|d(rpm)/dt|` of **5,927 rpm/s** against **869 rpm/s**
for frames that agree — the two quantities are sampled a moment apart inside the
game, which only shows up while engine speed is changing fast.

Observed ranges: power −41,052 W to 250,352 W; torque −53.6 N·m to 411.2 N·m.
Both go negative, as engine braking should.

### Wheel rotation is radians per second

Dividing speed (a proven canonical value) by the value at `100 + 4i` gives a
length. If the field is angular velocity in rad/s, that length is the effective
rolling radius and should be a constant of the car:

| Source index | p05 | median | p95 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.3185 | **0.3247** | 0.3540 |
| 1 | 0.3188 | **0.3247** | 0.3545 |
| 2 | 0.3166 | **0.3232** | 0.3611 |
| 3 | 0.3158 | **0.3232** | 0.3611 |

It is a constant, and it is ~0.32 m — a correct tire radius for a car. The
spread at p95 is wheelspin, which is exactly where a rolling-radius calculation
should break down.

The two medians are *different*, and they split the four wheels into `{0,1}`
and `{2,3}`. That is the first of three independent axle separations.

### Suspension travel is metres, and the normalized channel is 0..1

The value at `196 + 4i` is an exact affine function of the value at `68 + 4i`:

| Source index | slope | intercept | r | max residual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.058905 | −0.032505 | 1.00000000 | 1.8e-9 |
| 1 | 0.058905 | −0.032505 | 1.00000000 | 1.8e-9 |
| 2 | 0.051912 | −0.025512 | 1.00000000 | 1.6e-9 |
| 3 | 0.051912 | −0.025512 | 1.00000000 | 1.6e-9 |

A correlation of exactly 1 with a residual at f32 rounding noise means one is a
linear rescaling of the other. The `68` block spans exactly `[0, 1]` across
14,237 packets, so it is the normalized channel; the `196` block spans a few
centimetres, so it is the physical one, in metres. The control test — normalized
index 0 against metres index *j* — gives r of 0.134, 0.618 and −0.046 for
j = 1, 2, 3, so the pairing is not an artefact.

The slopes also differ between `{0,1}` (58.9 mm of travel) and `{2,3}`
(51.9 mm). That is the second independent axle separation, and it agrees with
the first.

### Combined slip identifies the three slip blocks

`combined_slip == hypot(slip_ratio, slip_angle)` holds with a maximum absolute
residual of **8.95e-7** and a maximum relative residual of **1.05e-7** across
all 14,237 packets and all four wheels.

The cross-pairing control is decisive: testing combined slip at index 0 against
`hypot` of indices 1, 2 and 3 gives maximum absolute errors of 5.8, 11.5 and
12.5. So the relation not only identifies the three blocks, it proves that
index *i* refers to **the same wheel** in all three of them.

The identity is symmetric in its two arguments, so it does not say which block
is the longitudinal term. That is settled separately: predicting longitudinal
slip as `(ω·r − v)/v` from the already-proven rotation and speed gives cosine
similarity against the `84` block of 0.34 / 0.17 / **0.79** / **0.70**, and
against the `164` block of −0.14 / −0.26 / −0.02 / −0.03. The `84` block is the
longitudinal one.

Note what is *not* claimed: the correlation is strong, not unity, which is
consistent with these being *normalized* grip quantities rather than raw
kinematic ratios. So they are promoted as dimensionless values with no SI name
and no asserted domain.

### Tire temperature

The user directed this decision after being shown the evidence below.

The wire values span **141.13 to 334.55**. As Celsius that is physically
impossible for a tire. As Fahrenheit it maps to **60.63 to 168.09 °C**, which is
a correct operating range for a road/performance tire. Independent FH6 protocol
implementations document these four offsets as Fahrenheit tire temperatures, and
the documented Forza Data Out field ordering confirms the FrontLeft, FrontRight,
RearLeft, RearRight order (though it says nothing about the unit).

The conclusion therefore combines external protocol corroboration with our own
capture evidence, and it is explicitly **not generalized to any other
unverified FH6 field**. The conversion `C = (F − 32) × 5 / 9` lives in the FH6
adapter and nowhere else; the Fahrenheit wire value stays visible in
Diagnostics and in `sourceSpecific`.

One measured oddity, recorded rather than smoothed over: the two rear
temperatures are **bit-identical in 14,237 of 14,237 frames**, while the two
front ones are never equal. This is what the packet contains — it is not a
decode error — but it does mean tire temperature alone could not reveal a
rear-left/rear-right transposition. The other six per-wheel channels do differ
per corner, so the ordering evidence does not rest on it.

### Race time is seconds

Consecutive-frame deltas of offset 308 against deltas of the game timestamp at
offset 4:

| | error (seconds) |
|---|---|
| median | 1.8e-3 |
| p99 | 1.7e-2 (one frame at ~72 Hz) |
| worst | 3.2e-2 |

Cumulative drift over a whole capture is at most **34 ms over 82 seconds**. The
clock is in seconds and advances 1:1 with the game.

What is *not* established: what it counts. It ran from 33.6 s to 240.5 s in
these captures while the lap counter stayed at 0, so it is an elapsed-seconds
timer that is running outside a race. The canonical field asserts the unit and
nothing more.

### Vehicle codes, lap number and race position

These four configuration values and two counters are named by the FH6 contract
already documented in this repository, at offsets the adapter has decoded since
V0.4. Promotion adds no interpretation: a code is rendered as a code, and a
count has no unit to get wrong. RaceLab has no class, drivetrain or model
database and does not invent an enum label.

All six captures are of one car, so these fields have exactly one observed value
each (class 2, PI 600, drivetrain 2, cylinders 4) and lap/position were 0
throughout. That is weak *behavioural* evidence, which is why the manual
acceptance plan checks them in a real race. It is also why they are promoted as
codes and counts rather than as anything with semantics attached.

## Wheel order

Wheel order is safety-critical, so this section states exactly what is proven
and exactly what is not.

**Proven from the captures.**

1. `{0,1}` and `{2,3}` are separate axles. Three independent measurements agree:
   distinct effective rolling radii (0.3247 m vs 0.3232 m), distinct suspension
   travel ranges (58.9 mm vs 51.9 mm), and opposite response to longitudinal
   load transfer.
2. `{0,1}` is the **front** axle. The chain:
   - `+Z` is forward. Anchored on inputs whose meaning is already established:
     `corr(a_z, throttle) = +0.60` and `corr(a_z, brake) = −0.61`.
   - Body velocity confirms the axes: `max |v_x| / speed = 0.886` (lateral),
     `max |v_y| / speed = 0.033` (vertical), `v_z/speed ∈ [0.46, 1.00]`
     (longitudinal, never negative).
   - Increasing normalized travel means **compression**. In the six frames where
     vertical acceleration approaches free fall (down to −10.74 m/s², i.e. the
     car is airborne) all four travels collapse toward zero — 0.080, 0.084,
     0.162, 0.195 at the extreme, against a 0.53 mean. At the other end, the
     largest upward accelerations (+15.5 m/s², a landing) coincide with travels
     of 0.63–0.88. Across the whole dataset `corr(Σ travel, a_y) = +0.42`.
   - Under forward acceleration load transfers rearward, so the rear compresses.
     `corr(travel, a_z)` is −0.11 / −0.22 for `{0,1}` and +0.31 / +0.20 for
     `{2,3}`. So `{2,3}` is the rear and `{0,1}` is the front.
3. `{0,2}` are on one side and `{1,3}` on the other. Lateral load transfer
   splits them cleanly: `corr(travel, a_x)` is +0.50, −0.53, +0.49, −0.52 for
   indices 0..3.
4. Indices 0 and 2 are on the side *away* from positive lateral acceleration
   (load transfers opposite to the acceleration).

**Not derivable from telemetry, at all.** Which of those two sides is the
driver's *left* is a parity choice, and the laws of motion are parity-symmetric:
no amount of kinematic data can distinguish a left-handed world from its mirror
image. Determining it needs exactly one external bit — either a documented
convention or one in-game observation. No further capture analysis could ever
resolve it.

**How that one bit was supplied.** It comes from the documented Forza Data Out
per-wheel field order, `FrontLeft, FrontRight, RearLeft, RearRight`, which
corresponds to index 0 being on the −X side. This is not the forbidden
"the offset looks familiar from another Forza game" reasoning: every other
prediction that ordering makes — the front/rear split at `{0,1}`/`{2,3}` and the
same-side pairing of `{0,2}`/`{1,3}` — was independently measured here first and
matched. Only the parity bit is taken on documentation's word. A second
independent pointer agrees: positive steering correlates +0.53 with lateral
acceleration and +0.55 with yaw rate, which under the repository's existing
assumption that negative steering is left gives the same assignment.

The mapping lives in one constant, `adapters::fh6::WHEEL_ORDER`, and is asserted
index by index in `wheel_order_maps_each_source_index_to_exactly_one_corner`.
If manual acceptance shows a left/right transposition, correcting it is a
one-line change to that constant.

## Session summary

`SessionSummaryV1` is unchanged. In particular `distance_meters` is still speed
integrated over monotonic frame time.

The candidate replacement, FH6's own distance counter at offset 292, was not
adopted — and not merely because it was deferred. The two quantities do not mean
the same thing: the integrated value is distance covered during *this recorded
session*, while the game counter's reset and accumulation behaviour is unknown,
and in these captures it stayed at exactly 0.0 while the car was demonstrably
moving. Swapping them would have changed what the number means without saying
so. Summary semantics therefore stay as they were in V0.6.

## Frozen boundaries

Unchanged in V0.8: `ingress.rs` and `packet.rs` are byte-identical to v0.7.0.

- The units of boost and fuel.

## Manual FH6 acceptance plan

None of this has been performed. V0.8 stays unfrozen until it passes.

### 1. Overview — vehicle codes

Load any car and start driving. Overview → *Configuration* must show four
integers: class code, performance index, drivetrain code, cylinders. Check the
cylinder count against the car you chose, and check the performance index
against the car's PI in the game's own UI — those two are independently
checkable. Class and drivetrain must read as bare numbers, never as "A-class"
or "AWD".

### 2. Engine — RPM, power, torque

Drive through the rev range. Engine must show current/idle/max RPM, and power in
both kW and W plus torque in N·m. Expected sanity: peak kW should be roughly the
car's quoted power output, and power should fall to zero or negative off
throttle. Boost and fuel must still read "—" with an explanation.

### 3. Tires — four corners, no transposition

**This is the critical check.** With the car stationary and the Tires view open:

- **Steer fully to the right and hold.** Front-left slip angle should move
  clearly more than front-right.
- Then drive and do the following, watching which corner responds:
  - **Hard braking**: both *front* corners should show the larger slip-ratio
    magnitude and heat up faster than the rear.
  - **Hard acceleration from a stop**: the *driven* corners should show the
    larger positive slip ratio.
  - **Sustained left-hand corner**: the *right*-hand corners (FR, RR) are the
    loaded outside pair. Their temperature should climb relative to the left
    pair.
- If the left/right pair behaves opposite to this, the parity bit is wrong.
  Report it and the fix is one line in `adapters::fh6::WHEEL_ORDER`.

Also confirm all four temperatures are plausible Celsius (roughly 60–170 °C when
warm) and that the two rear values being identical is what the game sends —
Diagnostics shows the raw Fahrenheit readings side by side for exactly this.

### 4. Suspension — four corners moving independently

Drive over rough terrain and off a small jump.

- All four normalized travels must move independently, within 0..1.
- **Braking**: front travel rises, rear falls. **Accelerating**: the reverse.
- **Airborne**: all four should drop toward 0 together, then spike on landing.
  This is the same signature the compression direction was derived from.
- Travel in millimetres should stay within a few tens of mm.

### 5. Race — enter an actual race

Free roam is not enough; the lap fields were never non-zero in any capture.

- Race time should count up in real time and match a stopwatch.
- Lap should increment at each start/finish crossing.
- Position should match the game's own race position, and change when you
  overtake or are overtaken.
- Current/last/best lap and distance will read "—". That is correct for V0.8 —
  **please capture this session** (Diagnostics → Start Capture) so the lap-time
  fields can be promoted with evidence in V0.9.

### 6. Lifecycle — must not regress

- Short menu visit → status goes to GRACE → resume driving → **same session ID**.
- Long menu visit → session completes.
- Diagnostics must report recorder drops **0**, receive errors **0**, hub
  subscriber drops **0**.

### 7. Persistence — both schema versions

- Open the session you just recorded. It must show a summary, and its manifest
  must say `telemetry_frame_schema_version: 2`.
- Open a session recorded under V0.6/V0.7. It must still list and open, with its
  summary intact and `telemetry_frame_schema_version: 1`.
- Neither old file may be modified: check the modification timestamps.

### 8. Diagnostics — raw data still there

Confirm Diagnostics still shows the raw gear code, the per-wheel channels in
packet-offset order (not by corner), raw boost/fuel, the raw lap fields and the
vehicle codes.

### Reporting back

Anything that disagrees with the above is a finding, not a formality. In
particular a corner transposition, an implausible temperature, or a power figure
off by an order of magnitude each invalidate a specific promotion in the audit
table and should be reported as such.
