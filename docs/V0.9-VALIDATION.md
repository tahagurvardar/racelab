# V0.9 — derived analysis: evidence and validation

The evidence record for the [V0.9 analysis layer](V0.9-ANALYSIS.md): what was
built, what was tested, what real sessions produced, and what is still unproven.

**V0.9 is released as `0.9.0`.** The sections up to
[the hardening pass](#v09-hardening-pass--first-real-fh6-acceptance) come from
synthetic test sequences and offline analysis of already-recorded sessions. One
real FH6 acceptance drive has been performed; what it found, and what was
changed in response, is recorded in that section, and the release is made on the
strength of it.

One validation was **not** performed and is carried forward as a known risk:
[cross-car slip-threshold portability](#carried-into-v010-cross-car-slip-threshold-portability)
has never been checked against a second vehicle. It is listed in
[the roadmap](ROADMAP.md) as V0.10 work.

## Design decisions that follow from evidence

### Longitudinal acceleration is not read from the acceleration vector

V0.8 established `+Z` is forward with `corr(a_z, throttle) = +0.60` and
`corr(a_z, brake) = −0.61`. That was a **correlation used to identify a field
during an audit**, and V0.8 is explicit that correlations used for
identification never became runtime rules.

So V0.9 derives longitudinal acceleration as `Δspeed / Δt` over ≥ 250 ms. Both
inputs are proven canonical quantities and the derivative asserts nothing about
vehicle axes. The acceleration vector is not consulted.

### Slip sign is preserved and not interpreted

V0.8 proved the `84` block is the longitudinal slip term (cosine similarity
0.79 / 0.70 against `(ωr − v)/v` on the rear wheels) and that combined slip is
`hypot(ratio, angle)` to 9e-7. It proved nothing about what the **sign** of the
slip ratio means physically.

So `high_slip_ratio` reports `signed_peak` and calls it a slip ratio. It does
not say wheelspin or wheel lock.

### Suspension direction *is* established, so direction words are allowed

V0.8 proved normalized travel spans exactly `[0, 1]`, that it is an exact affine
rescaling of the metres channel (r = 1.00000000, residual at f32 rounding
noise), and that **increasing travel means compression** — from six near-free-fall
frames (down to −10.74 m/s² vertical) where all four travels collapse toward
zero, against landings at +15.5 m/s² coinciding with travels of 0.63–0.88, and
`corr(Σ travel, a_y) = +0.42` across the dataset.

So "high compression" and "high extension" are supported statements. "Bottoming
out" is not, and is not used.

### Turn sign is not mapped to left or right

V0.8 established that the parity bit — which physical side is the driver's left
— is *provably not derivable from kinematics*, because the laws of motion are
parity-symmetric. It supplied that bit for the wheel order from documented Forza
field ordering, and noted that the repository's steering-sign convention is an
assumption, not a measurement.

Yaw sign faces exactly the same problem and has no equivalent external
corroboration. So `signed_yaw_change_rad` is stored, shown in degrees with its
sign, and labelled **"Turn segment N"** — never left or right.

### The speed-discontinuity rule came from real data

Analysing real session `d69a…-3` produced a derived
**max deceleration of −203.28 m/s²**, about 20 g. That is a true derivative of
the recorded speed and a false statement about how the car was driven: a
respawn, a rewind, fast travel or a collision moves the car's speed without the
car having accelerated.

RaceLab does not guess which. A |Δspeed/Δt| beyond **50 m/s² (≈5.1 g)** is
counted in `data_quality.speed_discontinuities` and excluded from acceleration
events and summary extremes. Nothing is clamped and nothing is repaired.

After the rule, the same session reports a max deceleration of −49.45 m/s² and
82 discontinuities. Values sitting just under the bound are plausibly light
impacts; RaceLab reports the number and does not classify it.

### One intermittent failure, recorded

On the first full run after the new suite landed,
`session_recorder::an_interrupted_session_keeps_its_real_checkpointed_counts_and_never_completes`
failed once. It passed in isolation immediately afterwards and has passed on
every run since.

That test polls for a checkpointed manifest within a 10-second budget while a
120 ms checkpoint cadence runs. It cannot be affected by V0.9 logically: the
recorder test binary never constructs an `AnalysisRunner`, and the completion
hook only fires for a session whose status is `Completed`, while that test's
session is interrupted. What V0.9 did add was a ninth test binary running in
parallel, and its queue-bound test was writing 64 full-length sessions at once —
a disk-I/O burst large enough to starve a timing budget on this machine.

The fix was to shrink that burst rather than to relax the existing test: the
queue-bound test is about the queue bound, not about analysis content, so it now
writes 8-frame sessions. Four consecutive full-suite runs afterwards were clean.
The failure is recorded here rather than omitted, because a test that failed
once may fail again on a slower machine.

### Inspection for absurdity

Event durations by kind on `d69a…-3` (560 events over 142.9 s of analysed time):

| Kind | n | min | median | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `full_throttle` | 7 | 5,138 ms | 20,645 ms | 20,739 ms |
| `braking` | 2 | 111 ms | 1,111 ms | 1,111 ms |
| `hard_braking` | 2 | 111 ms | 1,111 ms | 1,111 ms |
| `rapid_throttle_lift` | 7 | 13 ms | 14 ms | 36 ms |
| `strong_acceleration` | 35 | 84 ms | 405 ms | 6,029 ms |
| `strong_deceleration` | 28 | 83 ms | 250 ms | 1,597 ms |
| `high_slip_ratio` | 87 | 123 ms | 393 ms | 4,836 ms |
| `high_combined_slip` | 106 | 121 ms | 306 ms | 5,282 ms |
| `high_suspension_compression` | 103 | 81 ms | 127 ms | 401 ms |
| `high_suspension_extension` | 183 | 80 ms | 280 ms | 3,775 ms |

What this confirms:

- **Every latched kind respects its configured minimum.** The minimum durations
  observed (80, 81, 83, 84, 111, 121, 123 ms) sit exactly at the configured
  floors of 80 ms, 80 ms and 120 ms. Nothing shorter leaked through.
- **The only sub-80 ms events are throttle lifts**, at 13–36 ms, which is
  correct: a lift is instantaneous by definition and measures how fast the pedal
  moved. Its anti-chatter rule is the from/to pair plus the 500 ms cooldown.
- **Long full-throttle stints are real.** A 20.7 s stint in open-world driving
  is plausible; it is one event, not 1,500.
- **`braking` and `hard_braking` both n = 2** on this session. A superset with
  an equal count means both braking moments went past 0.80 — consistent with two
  deliberate stops in a free-roam drive, and with only 2 braking events against
  28 decelerations (the rest being lift-off, terrain and impacts).
- **183 suspension-extension events** is the highest count and the one closest
  to spam. Spread over four corners and 142.9 s that is 0.32 events per corner
  per second, which is what rough open-world terrain should produce, and every
  one of them cleared the 80 ms floor. It is reported rather than tuned away.

First events and turn segments on the same session were read individually and
are internally consistent: a turn segment's entry speed matches the preceding
frame, the minimum speed lies between entry and exit, and corner-tagged events
name a single corner.

**No threshold was tuned to make a capture look better.** The only threshold
change made after seeing real data is the speed-discontinuity bound, which was
added because a −203 m/s² reading was *wrong*, not because it was unflattering,
and it is documented as its own rule with its own counter.

## Performance

Single pass, O(n) in frames. There is no nested scan over history: the only
loops inside a frame are over four corners and over the rolling window, whose
length is bounded by time and hard-capped at 256.

| | Result |
|---|---|
| Throughput | **34,600–55,700 frames/s** (release build) |
| Wall time, 4-minute session | **281–373 ms** |
| Peak working set, 11,006 frames / 32 MB frame file | **4.5 MB** |
| `analysis.json` size | 97–201 KB |

Memory is bounded by construction, and the measurement confirms it: a 32 MB
frame file is analysed in 4.5 MB of resident memory, which is only possible if
the stream is never held.

No obvious O(n²) behaviour exists. The event lists are the only structures that
grow with the session, and they are capped per kind.

## Remaining limitations

1. **Nothing here is confirmed against a running FH6.** All of it is offline
   analysis of recorded data. Manual acceptance has not been performed.
2. **Turn segments are not track corners.** They are yaw-rate intervals. RaceLab
   has no track model and makes no claim that a segment corresponds to anything
   on a map.
3. **Turn direction is not mapped to left or right**, because the parity bit is
   not derivable from kinematics and has no external corroboration for yaw.
4. **Slip sign is not interpreted.** Positive slip is not called wheelspin and
   negative slip is not called wheel lock.
5. **A speed discontinuity is counted, not classified.** RaceLab reports that
   the recorded speed jumped; it does not say whether that was a collision, a
   respawn, a rewind or fast travel.
6. **Every threshold is a RaceLab heuristic**, chosen to be defensible and
   documented, not derived from any automotive standard. Different thresholds
   would produce different event counts on the same drive.
7. **Historical sessions are not analysed on startup.** A pre-V0.9 recording
   shows "Not analyzed" until something asks.
8. **The 250 ms acceleration window smooths short spikes.** A genuine sub-250 ms
   acceleration transient is attenuated. That is the deliberate trade for not
   reporting sampling noise as driving.
9. **`rapid_throttle_lift` has no minimum duration**, by design. Its guard is
   the from/to threshold pair and the cooldown.
10. **Suspension-extension events are frequent on rough terrain** — 183 in one
    142.9 s session. That is what the channel did; it is capped and counted, not
    tuned away.

## Manual FH6 acceptance plan

Not yet performed. **V0.9 is not frozen until this passes.**

### Preparation

1. Build and launch: `pnpm tauri dev`.
2. Confirm the UDP listener is bound (Diagnostics) and FH6 Data Out is pointed
   at the RaceLab port.
3. Note the wall-clock time you start driving, so event offsets can be compared
   against what you remember doing.

### The drive

One continuous session containing, in this order, with a rough note of when each
happens:

1. **Full-throttle acceleration** — hold 100% throttle from low speed for at
   least 5 seconds.
2. **Hard braking** — from high speed, brake to nearly stopped with the pedal
   fully applied.
3. **Rapid throttle lift** — at speed, release the throttle from full to zero as
   fast as the controller allows, and do it once only.
4. **Sustained left turn** — a long, steady left curve held for at least 3
   seconds above 20 km/h.
5. **Sustained right turn** — the mirror of the above.
6. **Deliberate high-slip manoeuvre** — provoke visible tyre slip (a handbrake
   turn, a standing start on loose surface, or a deliberate slide).
7. **Rough / bumpy terrain** — drive off-road over uneven ground for several
   seconds.
8. **Short menu grace** — open a pause menu briefly and return, staying inside
   the session grace window so the session is *not* split.
9. **Session completion** — return to a menu and stay there until the session
   completes.

# V0.9 hardening pass — first real FH6 acceptance

The first real manual acceptance drive was performed on session
`3d52a1aa298030b7f46c578d8c4b20ef-2`: 5,127 frames, 71.354 s recorded, 61.379 s
analyzed, no gaps, stream complete. **The analyzer worked and `analysis.json`
was produced automatically.** What the drive exposed was product-level noise,
plus one timing question.

| Observed | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 351 events, of which 220 were slip rows | presentation failure — detectors right, rows redundant |
| 59 suspension-extension events, many 100–150 ms | significance failure — transient unloads reported as events |
| 21 turn segments, two netting 2.3° and 3.3° | segmentation failure — yaw rate without net heading change |
| `analysis.json` appearing 55 s after the session ended | **not** a failure — queue wait behind a 78-minute session |

Nothing about ingestion, recording, the session lifecycle or the frame stream
was implicated, and none of it was changed.

## Root causes

### 1. Slip was presented per corner, per channel

One maneuver puts several corners over threshold on **both** slip channels at
overlapping but not identical times. The detectors were correct; emitting one
row each meant 82 `high_slip_ratio` + 138 `high_combined_slip` rows described
one 61-second drive.

Fixed by coalescing into [slip episodes](V0.9-ANALYSIS.md#slip-episodes), a
streaming O(1)-memory state machine — not a post-processing pass over a stored
event list. The per-corner detector counts survive as data quality.

### 2. Suspension had no significance rule

`suspension_min_ms` was 80 ms with no merge rule, so every transient wheel
unload on rough ground became a user-facing event. This was already visible in
the V0.9 pre-acceptance data — session `d69a…-3` produced 103 compression events
with a median duration of 127 ms — so the same rule applies to both ends of the
channel rather than to extension alone.

Fixed with a 250 ms minimum and a 150 ms re-entry merge gap.

### 3. Turn segmentation had no net-heading requirement

Yaw rate alone cannot distinguish a gentle curve from a second of steering
correction that ends where it started. The real session's segments sorted by
net yaw show the two populations separating cleanly:

| Net \\|yaw\\| (rad) | 0.040 | 0.057 | 0.116 | **0.253** | 0.372 | 0.381 | … | 4.518 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Verdict | artifact | artifact | artifact | real | real | real | | real |

There is a clean gap between 0.116 and 0.253. `turn_min_abs_yaw_change_rad` is
set to **0.20 rad (≈11.5°)** — above every observed artifact, comfortably below
the weakest real turn, and chosen to leave margin on both sides rather than to
sit on this drive's data.

### 4. The 55-second analysis delay

**Investigated, not guessed.** The manifest and file timestamps decide it:

| | Session `-1` | Session `-2` (the acceptance drive) |
|---|---|---|
| Frames | **339,454** | 5,127 |
| Recording ended | 21:16:25.786 | 21:18:00.176 |
| `manifest.json` written | 21:16:25.821 | 21:18:00.356 |
| Analysis finished (`analyzed_at`) | 21:18:53.921 | 21:18:55.261 |
| `analysis.json` written | 21:18:54.083 | 21:18:55.396 |

Session `-1` was a 78-minute, 339,454-frame recording that finished 95 seconds
before the acceptance drive did. Its analysis took **148 s** in the debug build
the dev app runs. The acceptance drive completed *while that was still running*,
waited **53.7 s** in the queue, and then took **1.2 s**.

Every hypothesis is settled:

| Hypothesis | Verdict |
|---|---|
| The job was queued ~55 s late | **No.** The hook fires inside `finalize`, and the manifest was written at 21:18:00.356. |
| Another queued job delayed it | **Yes.** A 339,454-frame session was being analyzed. |
| `analyzed_at` misreports completion | **No.** It is 135 ms before the file's mtime, which is the serialize + fsync + rename. |
| The completion hook fires later than assumed | **No.** Frame stream and manifest mtimes bracket it to ~100 ms. |
| Filesystem / wall-clock interpretation | **No.** `ended_at_unix_ms` matches the frame file's mtime to within 80 ms. |
| Another lifecycle issue | **No.** `hub.tick()` runs every 50 ms through `Appliance::tick → snapshot`, so grace expiry is not deferred. |

Measured directly, the same 5,127-frame session analyzes in **1.17 s** in a
debug build and **0.26 s** in release. Tens of seconds of analysis was never
happening.

The serial, bounded, single-worker queue is correct and is **not** being changed
— making analysis concurrent would put it back in contention with a running
game. What was missing was observability, so the analysis document and the
runner status now carry `queued_ms` and `analysis_duration_ms` separately, and a
test reproduces the exact scenario.

## Slip threshold portability

**This is the most important remaining limitation, and it is not fixed.**

The six episodes cover **52.65 s of 61.379 s analyzed** — 86% of the drive. That
is reported explicitly in Data quality rather than hidden, because it is the
honest reading of what the thresholds selected.

Profiling the canonical slip channels (`examples/slip_profile.rs`) shows why:

| | acceptance car (3631) | V0.8 evidence car (3134) |
|---|---|---|
| `\\|slip_ratio\\|` front, p50 | 0.022 / 0.020 | — |
| `\\|slip_ratio\\|` rear, p50 | **18.76 / 18.87** | — |
| worst corner, p50 | **21.88** | 0.196 |
| worst corner, share ≥ 1.0 | **80.7%** | 23.7% |
| worst corner, max | 76.01 | 123.28 |

The rear axle of this car sat above `|slip_ratio| = 10` for **58.7%** of the
drive while the fronts sat at 0.02. That is a rear-wheel-drive car spinning its
rears for most of a deliberately aggressive session — the analyzer is telling
the truth.

The data is genuine, not a decode fault: V0.8's invariant
`combined_slip == hypot(slip_ratio, slip_angle)` still holds on this session to
a maximum residual of **6.5e-6** (and 7.9e-6 on the V0.8-era car), so the
adapter is reading exactly what the game sent.

But it means a **fixed absolute slip threshold selects between 24% and 81% of
frames depending on the car and the drive**. V0.8's threshold evidence came from
one car; two cars now disagree by more than an order of magnitude.

**No threshold was changed for this.** Fitting the slip threshold to one car's
distribution is precisely the tuning this repository forbids, and one session of
one car is not enough evidence to design a normalization rule. The proposal for
a later pass is a per-vehicle or percentile-relative slip reference, and it needs
several cars first.

## Carried into V0.10: cross-car slip-threshold portability

**This is the one known risk V0.9 ships with, and it is unvalidated rather than
merely imperfect.**

RaceLab's slip thresholds (`|slip_ratio| >= 1.0`, `combined_slip >= 1.0`) were
chosen against the V0.8 evidence car, ordinal 3134. The first acceptance drive
used a different car, ordinal 3631, and the two distributions disagree by more
than an order of magnitude:

| Worst corner | car 3134 | car 3631 |
|---|---|---|
| p50 | 0.196 | **21.88** |
| share ≥ 1.0 | 23.7% | **80.7%** |
| max | 123.28 | 76.01 |

The same fixed threshold therefore selected 24% of frames on one car and 81% on
the other. The data itself is sound — V0.8's invariant
`combined_slip == hypot(slip_ratio, slip_angle)` holds on both sessions to
better than 8e-6, so the adapter is reading exactly what the game sent — and the
81% figure is a truthful description of a drive whose rear axle really was
spinning for most of it.

What is **not** established is whether a fixed absolute slip threshold is
portable across vehicles at all, or whether slip needs a per-vehicle or
percentile-relative reference.

- **Two cars is not enough evidence to design a normalization rule**, and
  fitting the threshold to either car's distribution is exactly the tuning this
  repository forbids. No threshold was changed for this.
- **The exposure is bounded and visible, not silent.** Every analysis reports
  `driving_summary.slip_episode_seconds` against `coverage.analyzed_seconds`,
  and Data quality shows the raw detector count beside the episode count, so a
  threshold that is not discriminating for a given car is legible in the UI
  rather than hidden behind a plausible-looking episode list.
- **Nothing else depends on it.** Slip thresholds affect the slip episode list
  and the per-corner peaks inside turn segments. Throttle, brake, acceleration,
  suspension and turn segmentation are independent of them, and canonical
  telemetry is not affected at all.

**Carried into V0.10.** Settling it needs several vehicles with different
drivetrains and surfaces, which is the same evidence base a second game adapter
will require anyway. Until then the thresholds stay as documented and the
coverage figure stays on screen.

## Next real FH6 acceptance test

V0.9 is released without this drive, so it is **V0.10 validation work**, not a
release blocker — but it is the drive that settles the
[carried risk](#carried-into-v010-cross-car-slip-threshold-portability). Same
procedure as the [original plan](#manual-fh6-acceptance-plan), with these
additional checks:

1. **Drive a different car from the first acceptance session** (that one was
   vehicle 3631). Ideally one with less rear slip — a front- or all-wheel-drive
   car on tarmac — so the slip threshold is exercised on a second distribution.
2. **Include a clean, grippy section** with no deliberate slip at all, at least
   20 seconds. Expect few or no slip episodes across it. If that section is
   still inside a slip episode, the threshold portability limitation above is
   confirmed as a product problem and becomes the next fix.
3. **Two distinct slides separated by several seconds of normal grip.** Expect
   exactly two episodes, not one and not twenty.
4. **A deliberate steering wobble on a straight** — turn the wheel left and
   right without changing heading, for about a second, above 20 km/h. Expect
   **no** turn segment.
5. **Two clear sustained turns in opposite directions.** Expect two segments
   with opposite signed yaw change.
6. **Sustained rough terrain** and, separately, **a single sharp bump**. Expect
   suspension events for the former and none for the latter.
7. **Open the session immediately after it completes.** Expect either the
   analysis or an explicit "Analysis in progress", never a blank or a zero.
   Then check Data quality: `queued_ms` should be small unless another long
   session was analyzing, and the analysis runtime should be well under a
   second for a short drive.
8. **Record the counts** — driving events, slip episodes, episode seconds
   against analyzed seconds, suspension events, turn segments — before changing
   any threshold, so any further change is a reviewed decision rather than a fit
   to a second drive.

Until that drive is done, cross-car slip-threshold portability remains
unvalidated and is carried into V0.10.
